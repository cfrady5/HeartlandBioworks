Indiana is home to a robust network of contract development & manufacturing organizations (CDMOs), labs, and digital assets equipped to accelerate commercialization of a wide range of biotechnology solutions. Heartland BioWorks seeks to support innovator access to Indiana's network of biotechnology drug substance manufacturers; industrial biotechnology precision fermentation manufacturers; formulation, fill, and finish drug product manufacturers for both biopharmaceutical and pharmaceutical products; and other contract or support organizations relevant to biotech development.

BioCAN Grants will offset project costs for late-stage development, clinical, and commercialization activities that utilize these resources in Indiana.

The U.S. Economic Development Administration has approved deployment of $9,000,000 of non-dilutive fixed amount awards as BioCAN Grants over the next several years.

Individual grants range from $50,000 to $500,000, with an expected total of $500,000 to $1,000,000 in awards per funding cycle.

Organizations must include:

  • for-profit, nonprofit, or academic institutions with a registered legal entity in the United States
  • active SAM.gov registration and Unique Entity Identifier (UEI)
  • organizations generally classified as small businesses, generally fewer than 1,000 employees; defer to Program Guidelines for exact criteria

Eligible technologies/solutions include:

  • biotechnology — human, animal, or plant
  • advanced biomanufacturing innovations
  • software
  • hardware or equipment
  • material science
  • process innovation
  • drug delivery devices
  • combination products
  • analytical tools associated with biomanufacturing or biotechnology development
  • advanced manufacturing solutions associated with biomanufacturing

Ideal applicants include organizations utilizing Indiana-based CDMOs, laboratories, or other biotechnology resources (see the resource map).

Applicant solutions should generally be:

  • at or beyond TRL 6 — Prototype System Verified
  • locked into a lead drug candidate
  • in late-stage preclinical development with final preparation of IND filing for human biotechnology
  • completing GLP (Good Laboratory Practice) testing
  • completing engineering or GMP (Good Manufacturing Practice) manufacturing runs
  • initiating Phase 1 clinical trials
  • and/or demonstrating a prototype in the relevant environment

Priority will be given to organizations:

  • utilizing Indiana-based CDMOs, laboratories, or other biotech resources
  • with sufficient funding to sustain operations for at least the next 18 months

Meet Our Grant Awardees

Select an awardee to read what their BioCAN Grant supports.

LyoWave is advancing next-generation lyophilization through its qRF microwave heating technology. BioCAN funding supports a project with an Indiana-based CDMO to evaluate freeze-drying, thawing, and analytical performance — critical steps in scaling the platform for commercial use across multiple therapeutic modalities.

Learn More

Haima Therapeutics is developing SynthoPlate, a platelet-mimetic, peptide-decorated nanoparticle engineered to replicate the adhesion and aggregation functions of natural platelets. With BioCAN support, the company will advance manufacturing process optimization and tech-transfer activities with Indiana-based CDMO partners, laying the groundwork for scaled production and future clinical development.

Learn More

64x Bio is leveraging its VectorSelect platform and proprietary cellular barcoding technology to accelerate the high-throughput discovery and engineering of viral vectors for gene therapy. With BioCAN support, the company will work with a CDMO in Indiana to generate and test key production cell banks, advancing its platform toward commercial-ready manufacturing pathways.

Learn More

Neela Therapeutics is developing a novel AAV-delivered RNA therapeutic aimed at restoring meCP2 function for patients with Rett syndrome. Through the BioCAN Grant, Neela will partner with an Indiana-based CDMO to prepare for GMP manufacturing of material intended to support future clinical studies.

Learn More

POP Biotechnologies is developing its SNAP platform, which uses CoPoP-based liposome systems to transform recombinant proteins into self-assembling immunogenic nanoparticles. BioCAN funding will support GMP manufacturing of critical liposome intermediates in partnership with a CDMO's Indiana facility.
